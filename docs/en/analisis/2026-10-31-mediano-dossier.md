<h1>The Framing: Sánchez applies the "protective shield" of the "Marlaska doctrine" with Puente</h1>

<p>In today's jungle of headlines, the Government has decided to activate a new ideological defense mechanism: the so-called "protective shield" of the "Marlaska doctrine." The move, orchestrated by Pedro Sánchez and his loyal collaborator Óscar Puente, aims to cover up a series of controversial decisions under the guise of national security and social stability. What is revealed through the different media spotlights is not only the political strategy, but also the constellation of persuasive techniques shamelessly deployed to shape public perception.</p>

<h2>News</h2>

<p>The official announcement was presented at a press conference where Sánchez described the "Marlaska doctrine" as an essential tool to safeguard the country's interests against external threats and internal disinformation. Óscar Puente, acting as the technical spokesperson, specified that the "protective shield" would act as a content filter and a guarantee of coherence in the official narrative.</p>

<p>Since then, the information has been reinterpreted by the media, each with its own agenda. Some have focused on the alleged need to protect national unity; others have highlighted the potential limitation of freedom of expression and the concentration of discourse in the hands of the Executive. The divergence of approaches has created a breeding ground for the public to navigate between blind trust and reasonable suspicion.</p>

<h2>Techniques</h2>

<p>Journalists at <strong>ABC</strong> have resorted to testimonials from experts aligned with the Government, presenting opinions that endorse the need for the "protective shield" without offering counterweights. This <em>testimonial</em> technique generates a sense of authority that, in the reader's eyes, legitimizes the measure.</p><p><strong>OkDiario</strong> has privileged <em>recency bias</em>, focusing its coverage on the latest events linked to the announcement and casting a shadow over the historical evolution of the "Marlaska doctrine." By focusing on the most recent events, the outlet intensifies the sense of urgency and immediate relevance.</p>

<p>In <strong>HuffPost Spain</strong>, <em>appeal to emotion</em> predominates. The articles rely on personal stories that would supposedly be threatened without the intervention of the "shield." The use of emotionality seeks to provoke a visceral response that eclipses critical analysis.</p>

<p><strong>El Independiente</strong> practices <em>cherry picking</em>, selecting data that favors the official narrative and omitting those that could question it. This partial selection creates an illusion of consensus that does not correspond to the complete reality.</p>

<p>Both <strong>The Objective</strong> and <strong>El País</strong> fall into <em>negativity bias</em>, highlighting the possible negative effects of the "protective shield" and minimizing any positive aspect. Constant exposure to adverse scenarios fuels a perception of risk that can be disproportionate.</p>

<p><strong>Le Monde</strong> opts for <em>omission</em>. The French coverage barely mentions the measure, limiting itself to a brief paragraph lacking depth. By not addressing the issue in detail, the reader is left uninformed about the true scale of the debate.</p>

<p>Finally, <strong>elDiario.es</strong> employs <em>fear appeal</em>, insinuating that the absence of the "protective shield" could lead to a loss of social control and the proliferation of destabilizing discourses. Fear, as a driver of action, becomes the outlet's main tool.</p>

<h2>Biases</h2>

<p>The combination of techniques and the selection of sources create a web of biases that distorts public perception. <strong>Confirmation bias</strong> is reinforced when readers look to media outlets that match their preconceived ideas, while <strong>availability bias</strong> is fueled by constant exposure to recent and emotional news.</p>

<p>Furthermore, <strong>polarization</strong> is intensified by presenting the "Marlaska doctrine" as a matter of loyalty or betrayal, reducing the debate to a simplistic dichotomy. <strong>Selective disinformation</strong>, resulting from cherry picking and omission, leaves gaps that the government itself can fill with official messages, closing off the circulation of alternative viewpoints.</p>

<p>In this scenario, the reader faces a tangle of influences that, without a critical eye, can lead to the uncritical acceptance of the "protective shield" or to blind distrust—both unhealthy situations for a participatory democracy.</p>

<h2>Conclusion</h2>

<p>The deployment of the "protective shield" of the "Marlaska doctrine" constitutes, beyond its content, a lesson on the power of media framing. The coordination between the Executive branch and spokespersons, coupled with the variety of persuasive techniques detected, shows how information can be shaped to serve specific interests.</p><p>For the informed citizen, the key lies in recognizing the mechanisms operating behind the facade of the news: uncritical testimonies, the pressure of the recent moment, the appeal to emotion, the partial selection of data, the overload of negativity, deliberate omission, and the use of fear. Only by dissecting these elements will it be possible to form a balanced opinion and avoid falling into the trap of ideological framing.</p>

<p>Ultimately, the "protective shield" not only protects a narrative; it also reveals the fragility of the information ecosystem when persuasion techniques are allowed to dominate the public agenda. The task of readers is, therefore, to keep their guard up and exercise the critical judgment that democracy requires.</p>

<p>This dossier has been prepared with the intention of offering a clear and unvarnished view of the media strategy surrounding the "Marlaska doctrine." The responsibility to continue investigating and questioning rests with each of us.</p>
