<h1>Dossier of the Day: The Framing: PP and Vox command a broad majority while the PSOE sinks below</h1>
<p>Today, the Spanish political scene has become a chessboard where the conservative bloc advances unhindered and the PSOE seems to have lost its way. In this dossier, we break down the latest polling data, media maneuvers, and the logic hidden behind the official narrative.</p>

<h2>News of the Day</h2>
<p>The Sigma Dos poll published by <strong>El Mundo</strong> shows that the People's Party has reached 31.1% of the vote, while Vox drops to 19.3%. Together they command 196 seats, a majority that "makes an alternative parliamentary arithmetic impossible." Meanwhile, the PSOE has fallen below 25% and 100 seats for the first time in the legislature, losing 450,000 votes. The drop comes in the wake of the Urda train derailment, which has halted the upward trend of Pedro Sánchez's party.</p>
<p>In the business sector, <strong>La Vanguardia</strong> reports that companies like Cottet Óptica are abandoning the AVE high-speed train, replacing trips with videoconferences or flights, citing the increase in travel time to "more than two and a half hours." Finally, <strong>InfoLibre</strong> denounces that data centers are hiding behind tax exemptions that "save hundreds of millions" for tech giants, depriving several local councils of revenue.</p>
<p><em>"There is nothing to be done, how are we going to ask for votes?"</em> – El Mundo</p>
<p><em>"Since the trip takes more than two and a half hours, it's not worth going and returning in a day; plus, you always have the doubt of whether you'll arrive at the scheduled time"</em> – La Vanguardia</p>

<h2>Techniques in Detail</h2><h3>Transfer</h3>
<p><strong>Outlet:</strong> El Mundo</p>
<p><em>“There is nothing to be done, how are we going to ask for the vote?”</em></p>
<p>The transfer technique consists of shifting blame or responsibility to an external actor (in this case, the electorate itself) to distract attention from the government's management.</p>

<h3>Diversion</h3>
<p><strong>Outlet:</strong> La Vanguardia</p>
<p><em>“Since the trip takes more than two and a half hours, it's not worth going and returning in a day; plus you always have the doubt of whether you will arrive at the scheduled time”</em></p>
<p>An alternative focus is created (the cost of the AVE high-speed train) that distracts from the central debate regarding the decline of the PSOE and the consolidation of the conservative majority.</p>

<h3>Card Stacking</h3>
<p><strong>Outlet:</strong> InfoLibre</p>
<p><em>“Data centers save hundreds of millions in taxes that are ‘lifelines’ for towns”</em></p>
<p>Selective data are presented (tax savings) without mentioning the negative impact on tax collection or local services, generating a partial view that favors big tech companies.</p>

<h3>Fear Appeal</h3>
<p><strong>Outlet:</strong> ABC</p>
<p>The article resorts to alarmism regarding artificial intelligence, describing it as the “production of artificial quadriplegics,” in order to provoke fear and associate technological innovation with imminent danger.</p>
<p>This strategy is detected by the use of exaggerated language and the absence of nuances or verified data.</p>

<h3>Name Calling</h3>
<p><strong>Outlet:</strong> Libertad Digital</p>
<p><em>“Mercedes has it screwed”</em></p>
<p>The use of insults or derogatory labels to delegitimize the director of the Civil Guard and, by extension, the PSOE, creates an image of incompetence without providing substantive evidence.</p>

<h2>Shared biases</h2>
<p>The analyzed media outlets share a bias toward reinforcing the conservative bloc and destabilizing the PSOE. All of them employ techniques that simplify reality, highlight specific failures, and silence broader contexts. This homogeneity works because the reader, saturated with information, seeks cognitive shortcuts: external blame, fear of change, or the demonization of the adversary. The result is a perception of inevitability that favors the narrative of the “grand majority” and the government's weakness.</p>

<h2>Conclusion</h2>
<p>The mechanism, exposed. Tomorrow they will do it again.</p><hr>
