<h1>How they sell defeat as victory in Congress</h1>

While the left celebrates its own funeral, the right is already carving up the cake of the new hegemony.
It is not a change of government; it is a change of the rules of the game in real time.
Here, they don't just play with marked cards—they burn the deck and invent a new one where there is only one color.
The absolute majority of the PP and Vox is not a statistical anomaly; it is the materialization of a political laboratory that has operated for years under the radar.

While the debate focuses on the polls, the media machinery has managed to disguise submission as realism.
They have turned surrender into a survival strategy, brainwashing the leftist voter into accepting their own irrelevance.
The only winner of this theater is the system, which has proven it can rewrite history without anyone raising a hand.

<h2>El Mundo: Transfer</h2>

<p><strong>Real headline:</strong> "PSOE loses 450,000 votes and falls below 100 seats in the first poll after the Urda event"</p>

<p><strong>Source:</strong> <a href="https://www.elmundo.es/espana/encuestas/2026/11/01/6ae77446e4d4d8f3698b458a.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Read in El Mundo</a></p>

<p>The fragment that gives away the transfer technique is the phrase "has had an immediate impact on the PSOE's electoral prospects." With this sentence, the media outlet directly links the railway accident to the party's decline, shifting the blame for the event onto the Government's management.</p>

<p>This choice gives me a feeling of blatant manipulation: the reader is forced to associate the disaster with the PSOE's incompetence, even though there is no evidence that the catastrophe is a consequence of political decisions. The tone becomes alarmist and the narrative turns into an electoral weapon.</p>

<p>To detect this in future pieces, it is enough to look for expressions that connect an event outside the political sphere with a party's popularity or morality, using verbs like "impact," "affect," or "burden." When cause and effect appear without a clear causal link, the transfer technique is likely being applied.</p>

<h2>La Vanguardia: Diversion</h2>

<p><strong>Real headline:</strong> "Companies reduce the use of the Barcelona - Madrid high-speed rail"</p>

<p><strong>Source:</strong> <a href="https://www.lavanguardia.com/economia/20261102/11673495/me-fio-ave-trabajar-empresas-reducen-tren-crisis.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Read in La Vanguardia</a></p><p>La Vanguardia uses the phrase “the Urda event” without going into depth. It is a clear example of diversion: it launches an emotional headline that promises context, but the body of the text remains empty, distracting attention from the central issue—the deterioration of the AVE high-speed train—and leaving the reader without substance.</p>
<p>This move makes me feel like an appetizer with no meal: intrigue that fades away. The reader, lured by the promise of drama, questions the media outlet's credibility, as the word "event" sounds too strong without substance.</p>
<p>Detecting it is simply a matter of looking for decoys: dramatic words that appear and do not connect with the argument. If a phrase seems taken out of another context and is not developed, it is a sign of diversion. The absence of data after the phrase confirms the detour. In addition, the lack of figures, quotes, or references backing up the mention increases the suspicion that the media outlet is only seeking to capture attention, not to inform.</p>

<h2>InfoLibre: Card Stacking</h2>

<p><strong>Real headline:</strong> "Data centers save hundreds of millions in taxes that are a 'lifeline' for towns"</p>

<p><strong>Source:</strong> <a href="https://www.infolibre.es/medioambiente/centros-datos-ahorran-cientos-millones-impuestos-salvavidas-pueblos_1_2239357.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Read in InfoLibre</a></p>

<p>The trick lies in the phrase "<em>lifeline</em> for towns", a word that, just like that, paints data centers as invisible heroes. By placing that adjective, the media shifts the discussion from the fiscal deficit to altruism, disguising tax evasion as an act of kindness. This choice provokes a feeling of complicity, as if the reader agreed with the idea that taxes are an unnecessary burden when it comes to tech giants.</p>

<p>To detect it next time, one only needs to look for words laden with positive or negative values that are not supported by data, and observe how the trade-offs are omitted. The "lifelines" are a sign of Card Stacking: one benefit is highlighted while the consequences of the exemption are eclipsed, building a one-way narrative that leaves no room for criticism or opposing evidence.</p>

<h2>ABC: Fear Appeal</h2>

<p><strong>Real headline:</strong> "Gonçalo M. Tavares, 2026 Formentor Prize: 'Artificial intelligence produces artificial quadriplegics'"</p>

<p><strong>Source:</strong> <a href="https://www.abc.es/cultura/goncalo-tavares-premio-formentor-2026-ia-produce-20261101014150-nt.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Read in ABC</a></p>

<p>The weapon of alarm lies in the phrase that ABC places at the beginning: "Artificial intelligence produces artificial quadriplegics". That phrase, laden with visceral imagery and an implicit threat, is the key piece of the Fear Appeal technique.</p>
<p>Upon reading it, adrenaline rises without the reader being aware of it: the idea that the most promising technology of our era could turn us into "artificial quadriplegics" generates a tremor of unease that translates into panic.</p>
<p>What makes this appeal to fear effective is its concreteness. It is not an abstract concept; a physical and permanent consequence is visualized, something the reader perceives as imminent and absolutely real.</p>
<p>To detect this tactic in the future, look for phrases that present a direct threat, that link the technology or event to tangible harm, and that use words that evoke anxiety without resorting to explicit violence.</p>
<p>Recognizing this pattern makes it possible to unmask alarmist discourse and prevent emotion from being the sole guide for interpreting the message.</p><h2>elDiario.es: Omission</h2>

<p><strong>Real headline:</strong> "N/A"</p>

<p><strong>Source:</strong> <a href="https://www.eldiario.es/catalunya/gobierno-anticipara-indemnizaciones-doblara-ayudas-obligatorias-afectados-accidente-urda_1_13631241.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Read on elDiario.es</a></p>

<p>The omission technique is revealed in the phrase "the legal package will include doubling the aid provided for in mandatory insurance." By mentioning the expansion without specifying amounts, dates, or criteria, the text leaves the reader in the dark regarding the magnitude of the support.</p>
<p>This choice evokes a feeling of deliberate evasion; the narrative becomes a speech of cosmetic politics rather than a concrete report.</p>
<p>To detect it next time, look for phrases that introduce measures or figures but lack essential data. Notice whether the author mentions procedures without detailing requirements or consequences, or if they focus on the action without revealing its true scope. The absence of concrete figures or historical context is usually the key sign of omission.</p>
<p>Thus, the media outlet constructs a narrative that, far from informing, plays with the lack of detail, leaving the public waiting for figures that never arrive, and reinforcing the sense that the government prefers words over precision.</p>

<h2>BBC World: Framing</h2>

<p><strong>Real headline:</strong> "N/A"</p>

<p><strong>Source:</strong> <a href="https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/videos/cqvgjljj74n8o?at_medium=RSS&amp;at_campaign=rss" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Read on BBC World</a></p>

<p>The media outlet resorts to the phrase "rocked the party" to frame the story, turning the congresswoman's gesture into an almost epic event. By using that expression, the BBC suggests that Stevens' action is not just a political deviation, but a genuine internal revolution, a clash of titans that threatens the established order.</p>

<p>That linguistic twist leaves me with a mixture of disbelief and cynicism. The word "rocked" evokes the idea of shaking the earth, generating vibrations felt across the entire political stage, as if the party's stability were about to break under an unexpected weight.</p>

<p>To detect this framing technique in the future, it is enough to look for verbs that intensify the action and adjectives that paint the story as a catastrophe or a momentous event. When the text turns a political decision into "a clash of titans" or "an internal revolution," the journalist is already constructing a narrative that goes beyond simple facts.</p>

<h2>Libertad Digital: Name Calling</h2>

<p><strong>Real headline:</strong> "El DAO de la Guardia Civil confía en salvarse del caso cloacas del PSOE sin la directora: 'Mercedes lo tiene jodido'"</p>

<p><strong>Source:</strong> <a href="https://www.libertaddigital.com/espana/2026-11-02/el-dao-de-la-guardia-civil-confia-en-salvarse-del-caso-cloacas-del-psoe-sin-la-directora-mercedes-lo-tiene-jodido-7480561/?utm_source=rss&amp;utm_medium=feed&amp;utm_campaign=Libertad+Digital%3A+Noticias+y+opini%C3%B3n+en+la+red" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Read on Libertad Digital</a></p>

<p>The media outlet resorts to the name-calling technique with the phrase <strong>"Mercedes lo tiene jodido"</strong> ("Mercedes is screwed"), a direct accusation that reduces the director to a scandal figure without documentary support. By employing this vulgar insult, Libertad Digital reduces the complexity of the case to a pejorative label, leaving the victim without the opportunity to contextualize the accusation.</p>

<p>That insult provokes a mixture of irritation and distrust in me; the submissive tone suggests the outlet prefers sensationalism over veracity. It makes me feel that the piece relies on rhetoric rather than rigorous analysis, as if the audience were a public that delights in political drama.</p><p>To detect it in future pieces, look for expressions that replace facts with pejorative labels: <em>fucking</em>, <em>leaked</em>, <em>pressured</em>, or any phrase that subjects the victim to a qualification without evidence. Also flag the presence of uncorroborated anonymous sources and the absence of verifiable data.</p>

<p>In short, name-calling acts as a shield that protects the author from accountability, while the audience receives a simplified story that fuels polarization. Recognizing it means demanding greater transparency and truthfulness in journalism.</p>
