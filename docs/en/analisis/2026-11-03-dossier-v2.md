<h1>Dossier of the day: Feijóo: "Spain is in a state of emergency and needs people who know what they are doing" and the machinery of distraction</h1>

<p>The air smells of electoral gunpowder and derailed trains. In a country where reality is negotiated depending on who holds the megaphone, today's menu is varied: from the express nationality of a former foreign president's wife to hunting the hunter in a railway catastrophe. While Feijóo speaks of an emergency, the media sells us loose pieces of a puzzle. Here we do not just inform; we uncover the gears. Welcome to the circus.</p>

<h2>News of the day</h2>

<p>The central stage is painted by the disaster in Urda. <strong>Óscar Puente</strong>, Minister of Transport, faces a "hostile" Congress demanding his head. According to <em>elDiario.es</em>, the man went so far as to "place his position at Pedro Sánchez's disposal," but the boss told him to stay. The official narrative revolves around a "human error," a sticky label that <em>Libertad Digital</em> dismantles by pointing out that the Government seeks to "reduce what happened to a simple 'human error'," minimizing the "systemic security failure." Air traffic controllers fear for the identity of the designated operator, recalling the Überlingen case where an air traffic controller was killed by a victim. It is the classic mechanism: sacrifice the pawn to save the king.</p>

<p>Meanwhile, on the diplomatic and legal front, <em>The Objective</em> reveals that Beatriz Gutiérrez Müller, wife of López Obrador, received "preferential treatment" for her Spanish nationality. Consular sources ensure that officials made efforts to obtain documentation that the applicant "had refused to provide." A handwritten note in the file reveals the difficulties: "Presents father's extract certificate – Spanish line, but does not want to bring a true copy of the book." There is no mystery here, just revolving-door bureaucracy.</p>

<p>And to top it off, <em>InfoLibre</em> tells how Philippe Solomon, accused of pandemic fraud, avoids the judge by using a "supposed Ukrainian international arrest warrant" as an excuse. Five years later, the central piece remains elusive. And in the background, the BBC reminds us that the United States strikes Iran before the elections, with Trump saying he is in "no rush." The world is burning, and we provide the spice.</p>

<h2>The techniques in detail</h2>

<p><strong>Card Stacking (Stacking cards in favor)</strong><br>
<em>Media Outlet:</em> The Objective and elDiario.es.<br>
<em>Detection:</em> Facts are presented selectively so that only what favors the thesis is seen. In the case of The Objective, the verbatim quote "received preferential treatment during processing" is presented as an accomplished and exceptional fact, ignoring that the consular sources are "assurers" and not direct witnesses of the illegality. ElDiario.es stacks the cards by saying that Puente faces a "hostile Congress" and that he was "emotionally broken," a subjective description that is not supported by medical evidence, but rather by "sources close to him." By omitting technical neutrality and emphasizing political pressure, the reader concludes that resignation is the only moral way out, even if the law says otherwise.</p>

<p><strong>Fear Appeal (Appeal to fear)</strong><br>
<em>Media Outlet:</em> Libertad Digital.<br>
<em>Detection:</em> The brain's amygdala is activated before the prefrontal cortex. The phrase "they fear their identity will end up being leaked... could be in danger" and the allusion to "public lynching" and the Überlingen case where a controller was murdered do not inform about the accident, but rather generate terror. The quote "the controllers themselves have asked politicians for restraint" is used to paint the Government as a predator looking for quick culprits. By connecting an administrative failure with the possibility of physical violence and incident (implicit in the historical reference), the outlet forces you to choose a side: you against the State that protects the guilty party. It is pure manipulation through threat association.</p><p><strong>Name Calling (Name-calling / Negative labeling)</strong><br>
<em>Outlet:</em> InfoLibre.<br>
<em>Detection:</em> The label is used to replace the argument. The headline speaks of "swindling", but the body of the text describes an ongoing judicial process. The quotes "elusive businessman" and "avoids the judge again" are value judgments, not facts. By describing the accused as someone who uses "Ukraine as an excuse", the outlet saves itself the trouble of explaining the legal complexities of international warrants. The technique works because the adjective "elusive" predisposes the reader to see any legal move as a trap, invalidating the presumption of innocence before the judge even opens their mouth. It is the power of the word to define reality before the evidence does.</p>

<p><strong>Negativity Bias (Negativity bias)</strong><br>
<em>Outlet:</em> BBC World.<br>
<em>Detection:</em> What kills, destroys, or scares is prioritized, ignoring what builds or stabilizes. The news story focuses on the "strikes" (hits) and on Trump's phrase "We crippled them financially" (We have destroyed them financially). Mention that there are "very intensive" diplomatic talks ("very intensive talks") that could prevent a greater escalation is omitted. By highlighting the phrase "is in no hurry" while framing it in the context of an imminent military attack, the outlet reinforces the perception of uncontrollable chaos. The technique exploits the human propensity to pay more attention to threats than to opportunities for peace, ensuring that the reader feels anxiety rather than hope.</p>

<h2>The shared biases</h2>

<p>What pattern unites these disparate outlets? The <strong>personalization of the systemic</strong>. In Urda, there is no talk of infrastructure, nor investment, nor maintenance; there is talk of "the operator" and "Puente". On nationality, there is no talk of foreign policy, but rather of "López Obrador's wife". In the Solomon case, there is no talk of administrative transparency, but rather of an "elusive businessman".</p>

<p>The mechanism is brilliant in its crudeness: by turning structural problems into individual conflicts, an identifiable villain is created. It is easier to hate a minister or a businessman than a railway management system or a consular bureaucracy. Moreover, they all share the <strong>false urgency bias</strong>. Congress is "hostile", the media are "terrified", the judge is "avoided". Everything happens now, everything is critical, everything is an emergency. This artificial acceleration prevents the citizen from processing information calmly, leaving them in a state of perpetual alert where the first impression (the most manipulated one) becomes accepted truth. It works because the human brain, under stress, looks for shortcuts. The outlet offers them the shortcut: this is the culprit, that is the threat, and you must feel outraged or scared. They do not ask them to think; they ask them to react.</p>

<h2>Conclusion</h2>

<p>While Feijóo speaks of people who know, those who manage the pairing of news know perfectly well what they are doing. They are not journalists; they are perception engineers. Today they have sold you fear over a train, scandal over a passport, and confusion over a conflict. The mechanism, exposed. Tomorrow they will do it again.</p><hr>
