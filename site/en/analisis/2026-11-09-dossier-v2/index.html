The media and their techniques

They do not read reality; they edit it to make it sell. Every media outlet has its scalpel. While some cut history to make it violent, others sew it with golden thread. The following table strips down the skeleton of the news day.

Media Detected Technique What It Omits (Strategic Silence)
La Vanguardia Appeal to the threat of loss of control and negative association with Trump. Concrete housing or pension policies; the complexity of the relationship with the U.S. beyond the "far-right" label.
ABC Positive framing (halo effect) and minimization of emotional impact. The concrete human pain of the affected families in Urda; the context of railway safety beyond the electoral figure.

The techniques in detail

Let's take apart the pocket watch. There are no coincidences here, only gears.

1. The Threat of Loss of Control (La Vanguardia)
Fear is the cheapest fuel in politics. La Vanguardia uses the figure of Trump not as a political actor, but as a destructive god. The actual text reads: "Sánchez warns that if the PP and Vox govern, they will bow down to Trump: 'These guys swallow anything'".
How to detect it: Look for the absence of nuance in international relations. When a media outlet reduces diplomatic complexity to "swallowing" or "bowing down," it is appealing to the reader's helplessness. They aren't selling you a policy; they are selling you the panic of being a vassal. If the headline associates a domestic party with a foreign leader without nuance, you are facing a campaign of fear, not information.

2. The Halo of Practical Management (20 Minutes)
Feijóo builds an aura of a technician against Sánchez's "actor." The exact quote is devastating: "Less looking at Trump and more at the trains, because this country is collapsed, it doesn't work."
How to detect it: Look at the change of scenery. Suddenly, foreign policy is "looking outward" (cold, distant, irrelevant) and the domestic is "looking at the trains" (warm, urgent, real). It is a contrast technique that makes the State's responsibility for infrastructure invisible and turns it into a problem of governmental attention. If a political leader is defined by what they "do not look at" instead of what they "do," they are using the halo of normality to hide the lack of structural solutions.

3. Dehumanization and Existential Urgency (Libertad Digital)
Here the technique gets dirty. The Urda event is mixed with the invasion narrative. The actual text: "Morocco invaded Spain through Ceuta... and Sánchez refuses to attend a Catholic funeral." And further on: "the remainder, some 15,000 criminals, continues to be controlled... by the Rabat police."
How to detect it: Look for the forced confluence of unconnected events. If a media outlet connects a train accident with a border crisis using warlike language ("invaded," "henchmen," "criminals"), it is creating an external enemy to justify internal hatred. The omission of verified facts and the use of terms like "invasion" to describe humanitarian or migratory situations is the ultimate red alert signal. They do not report the crisis; they use it as ammunition.

4. Stability as Anesthesia (ABC)
The cold data that numbs the conscience. "The Urda accident barely alters the blocks for 29N."
How to detect it: When a mass event appears only as a statistical variable that "does not move" the result, the media outlet is validating indifference to human pain. The technique is normalization. By presenting electoral stability as a natural fact despite the catastrophe, a sense of "everything is in its place" is generated. It is a positive framing for the status quo, telling you: "Don't worry, the system works, the numbers don't change." It is the perfect anesthesia for the electorate: become a passive observer of an event that, according to them, does not matter to the final result.

Shared biases

Why does everything smell the same? Because the mechanism is the same. Everyone, from the progressive left of Europa Press ("vote en masse") to the traditional right of ABC ("stable blocks") or the far right of LD ("invasion"), share a fundamental bias: the instrumentalization of fear or apathy.

On one hand, the fear of losing sovereignty (Sánchez/Trump) or the fear of cultural invasion (LD). On the other, calculated apathy (ABC), which tells you that chaos does not affect the electoral order. The repetitive pattern is binarization: either you are with us, or you are part of the chaos. There is no room for nuance, for Adif's technical analysis, for the complexity of foreign policy. There is only "swallowing" or "managing", "invasion" or "sovereignty".

It works because the average citizen is exhausted. They do not want to understand the railway system or geopolitics; they want a simple answer. Are you scared? Vote with us. Are you tired? Vote for stability. The media do not give you the tools to think; they give you the emotion to act. And while you act on instinct, the machinery keeps turning without anyone asking why trains crash or why walls open up.

Conclusion

The stage is set. The lights are on. Sánchez sold you the fear of Trump's backyard. Feijóo sold you the promise that he does look after the trains. LD sold you the conflict against the invader. And ABC sold you the peace of mind that nothing is going to change even if the world splits in two.

They all lie by omission. They all choose an angle so that the truth doesn't fit. They are not informing you of what is happening; they are telling you how you should feel about what is happening. And you, with your vote in hand, are the target of this bombardment of emotions. Think before you push the button. Or don't push it. The mechanism, exposed. Tomorrow they will do it again.
