Dossier of the day: The Framing: Vox nears a milestone with two seats in Galicia and boosts the right

November 29 approaches with a clash of forces that, according to reports coming from the media, will place Vox at the center of the political scene. With a jump of two seats in Galicia, the far-right seems to have found new fertile ground, while the PP prepares to take the contest to the right. This dossier dismantles the mechanisms used by the media to present reality as an inevitable story and, above all, reveals how information becomes a weapon of persuasion.

News of the day

The electoral landscape presents the right on the rise and the PSOE in decline. Two polls, one from El Español and another from OkDiario, paint a picture in which the PP and Vox unite to surpass the socialist block, while the illicit financing of the PSOE remains under the spotlight of Libertad Digital. Below is a summary of the most relevant data.

  • PP and Vox at the top: The El Español poll indicates that the PP has "an 88% chance of being the party that obtains the most seats and a 94% chance of being the most voted in the November 29 elections," and that the right-wing bloc, formed by the PP, Vox, and UPN, would gather "51.2% of the vote and 199 seats."
  • The PSOE in the shadows: According to OkDiario, the PSOE "stays at 104 seats compared to the comfortable majority of the PP and Vox," while the center-right "would achieve a comfortable absolute majority sufficient to form a government after November 29... up to 202 adding the figures of the People's Party and Vox."
  • Financing Investigation: Libertad Digital reports that "The head of Central Investigating Court No. 5 of the National Court, Santiago Pedraz, is investigating the alleged illegal financing through the SEPI plot."

These figures, although lacking historical context, create a narrative of inevitability that the media strives to reinforce.

The media and its techniques

The media do not limit themselves to reporting; they construct a reality that, once internalized, is perceived as natural. Below is a table that gathers the techniques detected in each media outlet and a brief indication of whether the explanation of the technique is omitted in the text.

Techniques in detail

Appeal to statistical authority (use of inflated percentages)

Medium: El Español

Direct quote: "Today, the PP has an 88% chance of being the party that wins the most seats and a 94% chance of being the most voted party in the 29N elections."

How to detect it: This technique consists of presenting absolute figures and percentages that, without context or margin of error, make the reader perceive absolute certainty. By not indicating the confidence interval or the poll's source, the number becomes a statement of inevitability.

Negative framing and power comparison (framing)

Medium: OkDiario

Direct quote: "The PSOE is left with 104 seats compared to the comfortable majority of the PP and Vox."

How to detect it: The weakness of the PSOE is directly compared with the strength of the PP and Vox, highlighting the difference in a way that makes the reader perceive a leadership crisis. Negative framing makes defeat appear more chronic and success more evident.

Use of negativity and sensationalism

Medium: Libertad Digital

Direct quote: "The head of Central Investigating Court No. 5 of the National Court, Santiago Pedraz, is investigating the alleged illegal financing through the SEPI plot."

How to detect it: The technique is based on highlighting serious accusations without providing concrete evidence in the text. The use of terms like "alleged" and "illegal" without detailed explanation generates alarm and doubt in the reader, while the media outlet positions itself as the bearer of truth.

Appeal to humor and fear to delegitimize regulation

Medium: InfoLibre

Direct quote: "More than 7,000 political ads bypass the ban by Meta and Google in Spain."

How to detect it: The technique combines a humorous tone with the denunciation of alleged technological censorship, generating a sense of rebellion against authority. By presenting the figure as a number of "bypasses" (mockeries), the media outlet delegitimizes the regulation while simultaneously encouraging subversion.

Shared biases

Although coming from different editorial lines, the media outlets share several patterns that reinforce the perception that the right is on the rise and the PSOE is in decline:

  • Focus on absolute figures: Both El Español and OkDiario use percentages and numbers without context, creating a sense of certainty.
  • Direct comparison between blocs: The contrast between the PSOE and the right is presented simplistically, reducing the complexity of political debate to a struggle of forces.
  • Use of negativity: The accusation of illicit financing in the case of the PSOE, without concrete evidence in the text, generates distrust and alarm.
  • Delegitimization of regulations: The denunciation of the ban on political ads on digital platforms presents the regulation as a threat to freedom, appealing to fear and rebellion.

These patterns work because they appeal to emotion and cognitive simplification. When readers lack the time or resources to analyze complex data, absolute figures and direct comparisons become the dominant narrative.

Conclusion

The mechanism, exposed. Tomorrow they will do it again.
